# Datasheet — released artifacts

A short datasheet (after Gebru et al.) for the data released with this paper. This release contains
**model outputs and human/judge adjudication labels**, not new benchmark images or questions.

## Motivation
Created to audit how multiple-choice benchmark *scoring rules* treat non-compliant model outputs, and to
anchor that audit to what a model actually answered. Released so any benchmark author can reproduce the
audit and check what their grader credits.

## Composition
- **Per-item model outputs** (`data/<benchmark>_<model>.jsonl`): for each item, the gold answer and each
  scoring rule's verdict on that item, plus the (truncated) raw generation. Five open VLMs
  (Gemma-4-E4B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B, Pixtral-12B, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision) across MMAD, MMMU,
  OmniMedVQA, PMC-VQA, POPE, ScienceQA, MathVista, MathVision. Includes the budget-64 robustness logs
  (`*_budget64.jsonl`).
- **Adjudication labels**: blinded manifests, `human_labels.jsonl` (130 MMAD, author),
  `human_labels_annotator2.jsonl` (40 MMAD, independent annotator naive to the hypothesis),
  `human_labels_pmcvqa.jsonl` (60 PMC-VQA), and `judge_labels*.jsonl` (Claude Opus 4.8, temperature 0).
- Each adjudication label is one of {a letter, NONE, MULTIPLE}, recording only what the model committed
  to, not whether it was correct. Human files store the short codes `N`/`M`, judge files the full
  tokens; `validate.py` canonicalizes both -- recompute agreement through it, or apply the same
  mapping first, or the vocabularies will not align. No personal or sensitive data; the underlying
  images are the benchmarks' own.

## Collection process
Model outputs generated by greedy decoding (temperature 0) at the budgets described in the paper. Human
labels collected with the released blinded MCQ tool (`src/adjudicate.py`); judge labels with the released
prompt (`src/judge_opus.py`). Annotator instructions (the rubric) are quoted verbatim in the paper's
supplementary.

## Preprocessing / cleaning
None beyond the join from stored responses to questions, which is verified against the gold sequence and
aborts on mismatch.

## Uses
Intended for reproducing and extending the grader audit. Not intended to train models. The labels reflect
what specific models answered on specific items and should not be read as general capability measures.

## Distribution & license

## Maintenance
Maintained by the authors. `validate.py` pins every reported number (33 checks); regressions surface as a
non-zero exit. Issues and contributions via the released repository.
